## batra-2005  Paediatr Anaesth 15(12):1094-1097
DOI 10.1111/j.1460-9592.2005.01633.x  PMID 16324030
PubMed publication type: Randomized Controlled Trial. Double-Blind Method.
MeSH: "Laryngismus / prevention & control".
PREVENTION, NOT RESCUE - CONFIRMED. Propofol 0.5 mg/kg IV or saline given BEFORE
extubation; extubation 60 s after study drug.
n=120 children ASA I-II, aged 3-14, elective tonsillectomy +/- adenoidectomy.
Result verbatim: "Laryngospasm was seen in 20% (n = 12) of the 60 children in the
control group and in only 6.6% (n = 4) of 60 children in the propofol group (P < 0.05)."
Conclusion verbatim: "During emergence from inhalational anesthesia, propofol in a
subhypnotic dose (0.5 mg.kg(-1)) decreases the likelihood of laryngospasm upon
tracheal extubation in children undergoing tonsillectomy with or without adenoidectomy."

## larson-1998  Anesthesiology 89(5):1293-1294
DOI 10.1097/00000542-199811000-00056  PMID 9822036
PubMed publication type: Letter. LWW section header: CORRESPONDENCE.
CITATION TRAP: LWW byline renders "Philip C. Larson"; the signature block reads
"C. Philip Larson, Jr., M.D." Given/family names are transposed in LWW metadata.
Notch location verbatim: "This notch is behind the lobule of the pinna of each ear.
It is bounded anteriorly by the ascending ramus of the mandible adjacent to the
condyle, posteriorly by the mastoid process of the temporal bone, and cephalad by
the base of the skull".
Technique verbatim: "The therapist presses very firmly inward toward the base of the
skull with both fingers, while at the same time lifting the mandible at a right angle
to the plane of the body (i.e., forward displacement of the mandible or 'jaw thrust').
Properly performed, it will convert laryngospasm within one or two breaths to
laryngeal stridor and in another few breaths to unobstructed respirations."
EVIDENCE BASE, verbatim: "Almost 40 years ago, Dr. N. P. Guadagni showed me a
technique ... which I have used countless times with complete success." and
"a thorough literature search has not revealed any mention of it."
Mechanism, verbatim: "The obvious question is, Why does it work? Unfortunately, a
sound, scientific answer cannot be provided."
NO DATA. NO CASES REPORTED. Personal practice only.
BONUS - Larson's own summary of the textbook sequence, verbatim: "standard textbooks
of anesthesiology suggest virtually the same sequence: namely jaw thrust at the angle
of the mandible while applying positive-pressure ventillation with oxygen, 100%, by
bag and mask and, if that fails, administering succinylcholine, the recommended dose
varying from 0.25 to 1 mg/kg intravenously or 4 mg/kg intramuscularly. [1-8]"
(sic: "ventillation" is the source's typo). This is Larson characterising textbooks,
NOT primary evidence.

UNPROVEN CLAIM, verbatim: "Although LNP is an accepted technique advocated by eminent

special jaw thrust) and simple jaw thrust (which may inadvertently overlap LNP)."
SEQUENCING, verbatim: "First, one has optimized the upper airway with routine

    WHAT POQI-3 DOES SUPPORT, verbatim:
     - "Practice recommendation 4: A structured bedside assessment should be carried out
       in response to postoperative hypotension/hypertension in order to (a) determine
       aetiology, (b) select appropriate treatment if indicated, and (c) consider changing
       the intensity of future monitoring and care environment."
     - "Hypotension should be treated immediately in the symptomatic patient. For a
       positive PLR test, intravenous fluid would be appropriate in many instances. If
       preload augmentation is not needed, vasopressor or inotropic support is indicated."
     - "Current evidence suggests that much postoperative hypotension is not caused by
       hypovolaemia, but treatment with a vasopressor or inotrope typically requires a
       high-intensity, monitored setting that has significant resource implications."
     - El Hadouti et al, prospective observational, recovery room, spontaneously breathing
       postsurgical patients with suspected hypovolaemia (systolic <90 mm Hg, MAP <70 mm Hg,
       oliguria, or HR >100 beats/min): "only 54% responded to the fluid bolus. This
       suggests that the typical approach of correcting postoperative hypotension with
       intravenous fluid (preload correction) may be inappropriate ~50% of the time, with
       correction of vascular tone or inotropy being required in the remaining patients."
     - Meta-analysis of 2260 patients: "the mean prevalence of fluid responsiveness was
       50%, and typical signs and symptoms of suspected hypovolaemia were not predictive
       of fluid responsiveness. However, an increase in cardiac output after passive leg
       raise (PLR) strongly predicted fluid responsiveness (positive likelihood ratio
       [LR]=11; 95% confidence interval [CI], 7.6-17; pooled specificity, 92%)." Negative
       LR=0.13 (95% CI, 0.07-0.22; pooled sensitivity, 88%).
    NET: a sourced *differential* for postoperative hypotension still does not exist in
    what is reachable. But POQI-3 supports a sourced *approach* (structured bedside
    assessment to determine aetiology; PLR to decide fluid vs vasopressor/inotrope), and
    it actively CONTRADICTS a reflexive-fluids framing. The card can be rewritten around
    that rather than left with six unsourced steps.
