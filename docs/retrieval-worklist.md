# retrieval-worklist.md

Full texts to obtain through the university library. Grouped by priority. Each entry names
exactly what has to be read out of it.

Everything here is a source I could reach a citation for but not a full text, or a source whose
number the day cannot be written without and where I could only see that number through a
secondary description.

---

## MUST HAVE
*The source carries a number the day cannot be written without.*

**1. Fu ES, Downs JB, Schweiger JW, Miguel RV, Smith RA. Supplemental oxygen impairs detection of hypoventilation by pulse oximetry. Chest. 2004 Nov;126(5):1552-1558.**
DOI: UNVERIFIED · PMID: UNVERIFIED · Day 3
Read: the Results section and the SpO2-versus-FiO2 figure. Confirm the proportion of room-air
subjects falling below SpO2 90% after the hypoventilation protocol, the corresponding proportion
at FiO2 0.25-0.30, the actual FiO2 levels tested, the duration of hypoventilation, and whether
subjects were volunteers or surgical patients. Day 3 has exactly one primary source and this is
it; every number currently attached to the day comes from secondary descriptions.

**2. Sessler DI, Meyhoff CS, Zimmerman NM, et al. Period-dependent associations between hypotension... a substudy of the POISE-2 trial. Anesthesiology. 2018;128(2):317-327.**
DOI: 10.1097/ALN.0000000000001985 · PMID: 29189290 · Day 5
Read: the definition of the exposure unit behind the intraoperative estimate of 1.08
(98.3% CI 1.03-1.12). The abstract truncates before naming whether this is per 10 minutes, per
hour, or per some other increment. Also read the POD1-4 estimates, which are reported on a
binary rather than a minutes exposure and therefore cannot be quoted in the same units.

**3. Liem VGB, Hoeks SE, Mol KHJM, et al. Postoperative hypotension after noncardiac surgery and the association with myocardial injury. Anesthesiology. 2020;133:510-522.**
DOI: 10.1097/ALN.0000000000003368 · PMID: UNVERIFIED · Day 5
Read: the table of absolute risk by MAP threshold and duration across the 50-75 mmHg range,
reported as cumulative minutes, continuous duration, and area under threshold. Confirm the two
figures now circulating in secondary sources: 2 cumulative hours below 60 mmHg in 8% of patients,
and 4 continuous hours below 75 mmHg in 48%. This is the only cohort with PACU-like sampling
frequency and it is the empirical backbone of the dose-and-duration claim.

**4. Botto F, Alonso-Coello P, Chan MTV, et al. Myocardial injury after noncardiac surgery... Anesthesiology. 2014;120:564-578.**
DOI: UNVERIFIED · PMID: 24534856 · Day 8
Read: the DOI (not surfaced anywhere in retrieval), and the table of 30-day outcomes by troponin
stratum. The headline figures are confirmed from the PubMed abstract; the stratified mortality
by peak troponin band is not, and that stratification is what makes the day actionable rather
than merely alarming.

**5. Apfel CC, Laara E, Koivuranta M, Greim CA, Roewer N. A simplified risk score for predicting postoperative nausea and vomiting. Anesthesiology. 1999;91:693-700.**
DOI: UNVERIFIED · PMID: UNVERIFIED · Day 9
Read: the incidence by number of risk factors present (the 0/1/2/3/4-factor incidence ladder).
The familiar per-factor increment is quoted everywhere but I could not confirm it from any
retrieved record, and the ledger deliberately does not state it. Day 9 cannot be written
honestly without this table.

**6. Apfel CC, Korttila K, Abdalla M, et al. A factorial trial of six interventions for the prevention of postoperative nausea and vomiting. N Engl J Med. 2004;350(24):2441-2451.**
DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa032196 · PMID: UNVERIFIED · Day 9
Read: the relative risk reduction per intervention and the explicit statement about whether the
interventions acted additively or with interaction. The additivity claim is the entire premise
of the day and it must come from the factorial analysis, not from the guideline's summary of it.

**7. Gan TJ, Jin Z, Ayad S, et al. Fifth Consensus Guidelines for the Management of Postoperative Nausea and Vomiting: Executive Summary. Anesth Analg. 2026 Sep;143(3):497-513.**
DOI: 10.1213/ANE.0000000000007816 · PMID: 41237407 · Day 9
Read: the rescue-treatment section in full, specifically whether the Fifth guideline retains,
softens, or drops the Fourth's position on using a drug class the patient has not already
received. The rescue rule is the second half of the day's claim and the guideline that carried
it has just been superseded. Also read the risk-stratification section to see whether the
four-factor Apfel score survives unchanged.

**8. Gross JB, Apfelbaum JL, Caplan RA, et al. Practice guidelines for the perioperative management of patients with obstructive sleep apnea. Anesthesiology. 2014;120(2):268-286.**
DOI: 10.1097/ALN.0000000000000053 · PMID: UNVERIFIED · Day 14
Read: Appendix 2, State of the Literature, and the evidence-category assignments attached to the
postoperative monitoring and discharge-timing recommendations specifically. The day is built on
the guideline's own hedging, so it needs the category labels (Category A/B/C, Level 1/2/3) that
sit behind each statement, not just the recommendation text. I have the key sentence verbatim
(see gaps.md) but not the evidence taxonomy behind it.

## SHOULD HAVE
*The day can be written from the abstract, but a figure or subgroup detail needs the full text
to state honestly.*

**9. Visvanathan T, Kluger MT, Webb RK, Westhorpe RN. Crisis management during anaesthesia: laryngospasm. Qual Saf Health Care. 2005;14(3):e3.**
DOI: 10.1136/qshc.2002.004275 · PMID: 15933300 · Day 2 · (open access, but the results table did not render in retrieval)
Read: the outcome breakdown among the 189 incidents. A secondary source reports desaturation 61%,
bradycardia 11%, post-extubation pulmonary oedema 4%, aspiration 3%, intubation required 43%.
Confirm these against the original table before any of them appear on a page. The 4% figure in
particular is the only quantitative link between laryngospasm and NPPE that Day 2 has.

**10. Sun Z, Sessler DI, Dalton JE, et al. Postoperative hypoxemia is common and persistent: a prospective blinded observational study. Anesth Analg. 2015;121:709-715.**
DOI: UNVERIFIED · PMID: UNVERIFIED · Day 4
Read: the proportion of hypoxaemic time that went unrecorded by intermittent nursing observation,
and the duration distribution. This is the cleanest available quantification of the gap between
what happened and what was charted, which is Day 4's structural-blindness claim.

**11. Driver CN, Laporta ML, Bergese SD, et al. Frequency and temporal distribution of postoperative respiratory depressive events. Anesth Analg. (volume/pages UNVERIFIED)**
DOI: UNVERIFIED · PMID: UNVERIFIED · Day 4
Read: first, the full bibliographic record so the row can be completed. Then the histogram of
episode onset times. The abstract gives median time to initial event 8.8 h [5.1-18.0], which is
after PACU discharge for most patients, and the day needs to be honest about whether the PRODIGY
data support a PACU-specific claim or a ward claim.

**12. Frank SM, Fleisher LA, Breslow MJ, et al. Perioperative maintenance of normothermia reduces the incidence of morbid cardiac events. JAMA. 1997;277(14):1127-1134.**
DOI: UNVERIFIED · PMID: 9087467 · Day 7
Read: sample size, the composite endpoint definition, the event rates in each arm, and the
temperature separation achieved. Needed to state the effect size, and needed again to set this
1997 result honestly against PROTECT 2022, which found no cardiac benefit over a 1.5 C range.

**13. Rajagopalan S, Mascha E, Na J, Sessler DI. The effects of mild perioperative hypothermia on blood loss and transfusion requirement: a meta-analysis. Anesthesiology. 2008;108:71-77.**
DOI: 10.1097/01.anes.0000296719.73450.52 · PMID: UNVERIFIED · Day 7
Read: the number of included trials, the pooled effect on blood loss and on transfusion, and the
temperature difference across which the effect was measured. PROTECT did not find a transfusion
difference across 35.6 to 37.1 C, so the temperature range this meta-analysis covers determines
whether the two results actually conflict.

**14. Devereaux PJ, Biccard BM, Sigamani A, et al. Association of postoperative high-sensitivity troponin levels... JAMA. 2017;317:1642-1651.**
DOI: 10.1001/jama.2017.4360 · PMID: 28444280 · Day 8
Read: the MINS incidence figure and its denominator. A commentary reports 17.9%; the ledger does
not state it. Also read the hsTnT threshold table so the day quotes the assay-specific cut-offs
rather than the older 0.03 ng/mL fourth-generation criterion.

**15. Ruetzler K, Smilowitz NR, Berger JS, et al. Diagnosis and management of patients with myocardial injury after noncardiac surgery: AHA scientific statement. Circulation. 2021;144(19):e287-e305.**
DOI: 10.1161/CIR.0000000000001024 · PMID: UNVERIFIED · Day 8
Read: the recommended surveillance population and interval, and the class/level-of-evidence
labels attached. The bibliographic detail in the ledger came from a secondary citation and needs
confirming at the publisher before it is cited. If Day 8 tells residents when to send a troponin,
that instruction has to come from here.

**16. Salzwedel C, Bartz HJ, Kuhnelt I, et al. Int J Qual Health Care. 2013;25:176-181, AND Salzwedel C, Mai V, Punke MA, et al. J Crit Care. 2016;32:170-174.**
DOI: UNVERIFIED · PMID: UNVERIFIED · Day 16
Read, for both: randomisation unit, sample size, the outcome measured (information items
transferred, not clinical outcome), and effect size. These two are the only randomised evidence
Day 16 has, and the day's word "measurable" stands or falls on them. Retrieve them as a pair.

## NICE TO HAVE
*Background depth only.*

**17. Blobner M, Hunter JM, Meistelman C, et al. Use of a train-of-four ratio of 0.95 versus 0.9 for tracheal extubation: an exploratory analysis of POPULAR data. Br J Anaesth. 2020;124(1):63-72.**
DOI: 10.1016/j.bja.2019.08.023 · PMID: 31607388 · Day 1
Read alongside the rebuttal (Fuchs-Buder and Brull, Br J Anaesth 2020;124:12-14, PMID 31685216).
Only needed if Day 1 acknowledges that the 0.9 threshold itself is contested. It is a live
disagreement and the ledger does not resolve it.

**18. Fortier LP, McKeen D, Turner K, et al. The RECITE Study. Anesth Analg. 2015;121:366-372.**
DOI: UNVERIFIED · PMID: UNVERIFIED · Day 1
Read: the Canadian incidence figure, only if the day wants a second national estimate beside
RECITE-US. RECITE-US alone is sufficient for the claim as written.

**19. Chung F, Memtsoudis SG, Ramachandran SK, et al. SASM guidelines on preoperative screening and assessment of adult patients with OSA. Anesth Analg. 2016.**
DOI: UNVERIFIED · PMID: UNVERIFIED · Day 14
Read: the methods paragraph describing how recommendations were formed in the absence of
randomised evidence. Day 14 is about guideline hedging, and this document hedges in a different
register than the ASA one, which is a useful contrast rather than a necessary source.

**20. Olsson GL, Hallen B. Laryngospasm during anaesthesia... Acta Anaesthesiol Scand. 1984;28(5):567-575.**
DOI: UNVERIFIED · PMID: UNVERIFIED · Day 2
Read: the incidence figure and its denominator definition, only if Day 2 states a laryngospasm
incidence. A 1984 Swedish cohort predates modern airway devices, so it should be flagged as
era-limited wherever it appears.
